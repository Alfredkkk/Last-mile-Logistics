4.5.1. Scaling with λ. Figure 7 depicts how the expected revenue rates of the optimal zoning
policy and the pure delivery policy scale with the passenger ride request rate, assumingn ={30, 50}.
We observe that the pure delivery policy performs optimally only under very low arrival rates. Once
the arrival rate surpasses a certain threshold, integrating on-demand rides and package delivery is
more efficient. The optimal zoning policy can take the form of LF before transitioning to the switching
policy, while the latter is more prevalent in the tested cases. Notably, the optimal zoning policy can
lead to 20-30% higher revenue rate than the pure delivery policy whenn = 30. We also observe that
the threshold forλincreases withn: as the package volume grows, a higher arrival rate becomes
necessary for the zoning policy to outperform the pure package delivery policy. Additionally, it is
worth noting that most revenue rate improvement can be gained with a small ride arrival rate. As the
arrival rate increases, the revenue rate of the switching policy grows at a slower pace as it reaches the
limit of co-modality (the maximum revenue rate of the zoning policy is limited by the revenue rate of
ride-hailing).
